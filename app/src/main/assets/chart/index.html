Where the data comes from

The chart in your screenshot was a TradingView-style chart with indicators from Velo (velo.xyz), which aggregates exchange data behind a paid API. This app rebuilds the same three panes straight from the exchanges' free public APIs:

Price: Binance USDⓈ-M Futures BTCUSDT perpetual candles (REST history + live WebSocket).

Aggregated open interest: BTC perpetual OI in coins, summed over Binance (BTCUSDT), Bybit (BTCUSDT), OKX (BTC-USDT-SWAP) and Hyperliquid (BTC). Binance only serves 30 days of OI history, so older days come from its public archive (data.binance.vision). Hyperliquid has no public OI history: before the app first recorded it, its earliest known value is carried back.

Cross exchange funding: each venue's funding rate in % per 8 hours (Hyperliquid's hourly rate × 8). Each bar shows the rate in effect at its close: settled rates for past bars, the live predicted rate for the current one, so the latest value is the same on every timeframe. OKX only publishes the last 3 months of funding history.

Order book heatmap (behind the candles): the look of Material Indicators' FireCharts (materialindicators.com), the chart in the HODL15Capital post. It paints Binance's BTC/USDT order book over time: resting sell orders (asks) in fire colours, buy orders (bids) in teal, brighter where more BTC sits. Material Indicators record Binance's book around the clock and sell that history. Exchanges only publish their current book, so this app records the books of Binance (spot and futures), Bybit, OKX and Hyperliquid itself, every minute while open and about every 15 minutes while closed, and adds together the ones switched on above. Bars before the recording started have no heat. Each bar shows the average quantity resting in each price band while the bar was open. Long-press to read it.
